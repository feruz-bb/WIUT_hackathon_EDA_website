Our Approach

Problem Understanding

A financial-sector monitoring unit generates automated alerts from customers' transaction history. Each alert is either dismissed or escalated for investigation. Our goal: predict the probability of escalation using historical transaction patterns.

EDA-Driven Feature Engineering

We performed deep exploratory analysis to understand behavioral patterns. Each signal has ~500 associated transactions — we engineered 246 features and applied rigorous feature selection to isolate the Top-75 high-impact features, eliminating noise.

Optuna Tuning & Ensemble

We conducted 200 Optuna optimization trials for LightGBM, XGBoost, and CatBoost, discovering that shallow trees and heavy regularization prevent overfitting. We ensembled 15 models across 5 random seeds, achieving 0.6505 ROC-AUC.

Key Strategy

Our analysis revealed that transaction volume, net cash flow direction, and recent activity intensity are the strongest signals for escalation. Escalated alerts tend to have more transactions, different incoming/outgoing patterns, and higher recent activity spikes.

Target Distribution & Class Imbalance

The target variable shows moderate class imbalance — only 17.2% of alerts are escalated. This imbalance informed our choice to use class-weighted training and stratified cross-validation.

Alert Classification Distribution

Monthly Escalation Rate

Analyst Note

Target Distribution & Temporal Invariance

The escalation rate hovers consistently around 17.2% across all recorded months without sudden seasonal swings. This empirical stability means the risk signal stems from individual behavioral anomalies rather than macro-calendar trends — which significantly reduces the risk of distribution shift between our training folds and the hidden test set.

Engineering Decision: Instead of synthetic oversampling (which distorts dense transaction counts), we preserved natural transaction distributions and countered the 1:4.8 imbalance directly in model loss functions via is_unbalance=True in LightGBM and scale_pos_weight=4.82 in XGBoost.

Batch Processing Artifacts & Pre-Alert Velocity

The sharp transaction spike observed at 23:00 (11 PM) appears identically across both escalated and dismissed accounts, characteristic of automated end-of-day banking batch sweeps rather than human fraud. In contrast, the true discriminative signal lies in the 30-day pre-signal window, where escalated accounts exhibit a 4.2% acceleration in transaction frequency (81.2 vs. 77.9).

Engineering Decision: Constructed dynamic pre-signal lookback ratios (w30_vs_90_ratio, amount_acceleration) to detect sudden activity ramp-ups against a customer's long-term historical baseline.

Feature Engineering & Modeling

Based on our EDA findings, we engineered 246 features from the raw transaction data across 8 behavioral dimensions, then applied permutation and split-importance selection to isolate the optimal Top-75 high-impact features, eliminating noise.

Basic Aggregates

Count, mean, std, min, max, median, skew, IQR, range, and coefficient of variation of transaction amounts

Direction Features

Separate statistics for kirim/chiqim, ratios between directions, net cash flow, and per-transaction net flow

Type Features

Per-type (karta, bank_otkazmasi, naqd, xalqaro) counts, amounts, and usage ratios

Time & Entropy

Activity span, recency, frequency, night/weekend ratios, hour distribution, day-of-week entropy

Window Dynamics

Fine-grained pre-signal transaction stats within 3, 7, 14, 30, 60, 90, 120, and 150-day windows

Behavioral & Sequence

Inter-transaction time stats, burstiness, direction switch rates, type change rates, and cross direction×type

Temporal Acceleration

Momentum features measuring transaction volume acceleration and amount deviation over time

Histogram Bins

Quantile amount binning capturing the distribution of micro vs. macro transactional activity

08

Conclusion

Key Findings

  1. Transaction volume matters: Escalated signals have ~6% more transactions on average (524 vs 494), making aggregate transaction count one of the strongest predictors.
  2. Cash flow direction signals risk: Subtle differences in the kirim/chiqim ratio (incoming vs outgoing transactions) help distinguish escalated alerts. Escalated signals show slightly different outgoing transaction amounts.
  3. Temporal stability: The escalation rate (~17%) is consistent across months, suggesting behavioral patterns rather than seasonal trends drive escalation decisions.
  4. Recent activity intensity: The 30-day pre-signal transaction window shows higher activity for escalated alerts (81.2 vs 77.9 transactions), confirming that recent behavioral spikes are informative.
  5. Amount distributions differ subtly: Escalated signals have lower mean transaction amounts (-0.116 vs -0.045), potentially indicating different financial behavior patterns.

Our Methodology

We transformed these EDA insights into a robust prediction pipeline:

  • Comprehensive feature engineering: 246 features engineered and refined to the optimal Top-75 high-impact features via permutation and split importance filtering, eliminating noise.
  • Optuna Bayesian optimization: 200 trials tuning LightGBM, CatBoost, and XGBoost, identifying that shallow trees (depth 2–3) and heavy regularization prevent overfitting in low-signal regimes.
  • Multi-seed ensemble: 15 models (3 model families × 5 seeds) combined with out-of-fold AUC weighting, achieving 0.6505 ROC-AUC.

The EDA-driven feature engineering approach ensures that every feature captures a meaningful behavioral pattern observed in the data, rather than relying on blind automated feature generation.
